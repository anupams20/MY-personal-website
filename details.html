For mental-health professionals

Psychiatrists, psychologists and counsellors get a different kind of website, because their patients behave differently: stigma means people search online privately, often for weeks, before they ever ask anyone. Your website is usually their first step — so it must feel calm and safe, not commercial.

  • Privacy-first design — discreet copy, a booking form where even the full name is optional, and an explicit confidentiality promise on the page.
  • Teleconsultation page — video consults lower the barrier for patients who won't walk in, and for follow-ups (dropout between sessions is the biggest revenue leak in psychiatric practice).
  • NMC-compliant always — no patient testimonials, no "best psychiatrist" claims, no discount framing. The NMC guidelines (updated 2026) prohibit these on doctors' websites; factual information — qualifications, services, timings, fees — is fully permitted. I build to the rules so your registration is never at risk.

Questions, answered properly

No — it's the normal situation. I write all the text for your specialty, use your Google photos and reviews where they exist, and you can WhatsApp me ten photos from your phone for the rest. Real photos of your actual clinic always build more trust than stock images, but we can start with tasteful stock and swap later.

I'm already on Practo/Justdial. Why do I need my own website?

Those platforms rent you visibility — next to ads for your competitors, on their terms, with their branding. Your own website is an asset you own: it ranks on Google for your name and specialty, carries your reviews, and sends patients to YOUR WhatsApp — not into a marketplace that also lists the clinic across the road.

Can my receptionist really use the OPD software?

Yes — it's deliberately simple: search patient, add visit, print prescription. I train your front desk on a call when we go live; most people are comfortable within a day, and I remain available on WhatsApp if anything confuses them.

Is this allowed under medical advertising rules?

Yes — if it's done right, and most local web designers don't know the rules. The NMC guidelines (updated 2026) prohibit patient testimonials, "best doctor" claims, discount offers and guaranteed cures on a doctor's website — but fully permit factual information: qualifications, departments, services, facilities, timings and charges. I build every clinic site to be NMC-compliant from day one.
